## 6. Programme Revision Lifecycle
When a new revised CPM (Programme Revision) becomes authorised:
- A new operational Site Diary cycle begins exclusively against the new revision's tasks.
- Operational operations on the previous revision end immediately. Previous Open Activities are locked (`is_locked = true`).
- Historical records remain preserved, immutable, and readable under their original revision.
- There is NO cross-revision migration, merging, or carry-forward of Open Activities.

## 7. Activity Lifecycle
Activity lifecycle consists of three canonical physical execution states: `New`, `In Progress`, and `Completed`.
- **Prohibited Transitions:** Administrative states like `Suspended` or `Cancelled` must not illegally bypass or expand these three physical execution states.
- **Lifecycle Rules:** Date-aware wrappers preserve the transition from `New -> In Progress -> Completed`, including Known Start Date and Actual Finish for same-day start+completion. Lifecycle rules are enforced at the engine level and must not be bypassed for UI convenience.

## 8. MSP vs VO Operational Source Model
Per ADR-F1-001, an Activity has exactly one operational source: either an MSP Task or a VO Item (XOR rule).
- **MSP-sourced Activity:** Derived from the current authorised Programme Revision Tasks.
- **VO-sourced Activity:** Captures legitimate work outside the current MSP.
- **Rules:** Fake MSP Tasks must not be created for VO. Historical source identity is immutable. If a VO's scope is later incorporated into an approved MSP revision, historical VO Activities are NOT rewritten as MSP Activities. Programme/Revision ownership remains mandatory in all cases.

## 9. Open Activities / Continue Yesterday
- **Open Activities:** A domain state concept representing an `Activity` that is currently operational (`New` or `InProgress`, and `is_locked = false`) within the current authorised Programme Revision. 
- **Continue Yesterday:** Eligibility is strictly limited to active, non-completed Activities in the current revision. It safely instantiates a new child `site_diary` entry for today, carrying forward permitted context (like `print_context`) but clearing day-specific time/weather values to prevent duplicate operational rows. Cross-revision continuation is banned.

## 10. Site Diary Mutability, Cardinality, and Workforce Integrity
- **Cardinality (DB-015 / AGENTS.md):** 
  - The relationship between Activity and Site Diary is 1-to-Many (`Activity 1 → Many Site Diary`).
  - For a given operational date, a Site Diary record represents one Activity on one day.
  - Database uniqueness enforces `UNIQUE(activity_id, activity_date)`, prohibiting multiple diary entries for the same Activity on the same operational date.
- **Governed Mutability vs Immutability:**
  - *Permitted Governed Operational Editing:* Site Diary records for an active Activity on its operational date can be updated (via PATCH / edit engine) to modify daily snapshot details (notes, weather, manpower, print context).
  - *Historical Identity Preservation:* Governed updates mutate the existing `site_diary` row (`site_diary_id` is preserved, `editingReportId === site_diary_id`), preserving relational links and preventing duplicate operational rows.
  - *Audit/History Preservation:* Mutations trigger append-only audit logging in `site_diary_logs` (every NEW creates a NEW event; every EDIT creates an UPDATE event). Historical audit log rows are strictly immutable.
  - *Immutable Architecture Constraints:* Historical records locked under superseded revisions cannot be mutated. Activity source identity (MSP XOR VO) is immutable once created.
- **Workforce / Trades:** Site Diary creation/updates utilize full atomic wrappers that synchronize the Diary snapshot with canonical Workforce rows per Trade. Legacy direct table mutation for Trades is replaced by authenticated atomic creation. Client-side direct table mutations remain revoked.
