## B01B.1 baseline versus B01C-A spike

### Baseline: B01B.1

The baseline is functionally clear and compact, but its source selector relies on a filled blue tab, generic rounded inputs, and individually rounded task cards. Repetition produces card soup, the task list scans as a collection of web cards, and press/selection physicality is limited.

### Spike: B01C-A

- **Premium feel:** materially stronger through disciplined matte layers, tighter edges, inset controls, typographic hierarchy, and quieter use of blue.
- **Tactical identity:** recognisable but restrained. The Operational Rail, command-style search surface, compact metadata, and instrument-like arrow affordances make the workflow feel purpose-built.
- **Field usability:** retained or improved. Source controls are 48 px high, task rows are at least 82 px high, critical task titles are 13 px on mobile, metadata is at least 11 px, and the bottom targets remain 56 px high.
- **Readability:** programme and revision authority remain fully visible. Task title, WBS/UID, dates, and actions separate cleanly without excessive type-scale variation.
- **Scan speed:** improved. Shared row framing, straight dividers, and consistent metadata positions allow multiple tasks to be compared vertically without parsing separate cards.
- **Card-soup reduction:** substantial in the target region. Five task cards become one operational list with divided rows; strong rounding remains only where it supports a touch control or engaged source summary.
- **Physicality:** improved through inset selected surfaces, active rail, 100–140 ms response, shallow depression, compact action keys, and a shared bottom-nav engagement grammar.
- **Excessive-gamer risk:** low. There is no HUD layout, neon, orange identity, decorative animation, sci-fi iconography, or game artwork.
- **Excessive-decoration risk:** low to moderate. The full-height source-region rail is the strongest signature element; it remains a 3 px state edge rather than a decorative panel treatment.

## Known compromises

- The spike intentionally stops at the New Entry source-selection viewport; subsequent form sections retain the existing visual system, so the transition becomes visible after an MSP task is selected.
- The long WBS fixture is truncated by the viewport just as an actual compact field list requires; the task name and row remain usable, and horizontal overflow is absent.
- The mobile bottom-navigation refinement is shared across its four canonical destinations because it is one shell-owned component, but no information architecture or destination semantics changed.
- The visual direction has been self-assessed only. Product Owner/HQ judgement on the RM99/month premium threshold remains pending.
